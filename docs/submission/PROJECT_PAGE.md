# Space Apps project page copy — PULSE

## Title

PULSE — Astronaut Health Monitoring for Earth-Independent Operations

## High-level summary

PULSE is crew-facing software that gathers health indicators, evaluates them against configurable thresholds and NASA hazard framing, and acts with receipted escalations — including store-and-forward when the Earth link is lost.

## Description (~220 words)

Deep-space crews cannot rely on real-time flight surgeons. PULSE is a NASA Space Apps 2026 prototype that implements the challenge’s three verbs — gather, evaluate, act — for astronaut health monitoring on long-duration missions.

The app streams simulated vitals for a four-person fictional crew, monitors radiation dose and cabin environment, tracks exercise/sleep countermeasures, and captures behavioral mood check-ins. Channels are grouped by NASA HRP’s five hazards of human spaceflight. A Named Risk Registry connects findings to published HRP risks. A BLIND quality gate refuses bad sensor samples. Every engine output and threshold edit produces an immutable receipt.

When the Earth link drops (LOS), escalations queue as QUEUED-FS and flush with sync receipts on AOS — demonstrating Earth-independent medical operations under delay and disruption. A DONKI-style storm replay shows solar-particle stress on the radiation channel. Nineteen in-app citations include the five official challenge resources (HRP hazards, risks, ongoing research; CSA radiation monitoring and space-science pages).

Simulated data only — demonstration, not a medical device. Non-public limits are labeled demo placeholders.
